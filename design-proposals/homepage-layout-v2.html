My Recommendation

Lead with Option C — The Dual Path

Your portfolio honestly serves two buyers, and forcing one hero means losing half your pipeline before they've read a sentence. Option C solves this without diluting the brand: the H1 stays confident and specific, then visitors self-select into the track that matches their need — and from that point on the page stays identical.

On SEO, Option C actually outperforms the narrower options for your situation because your authority signals (portfolio, LinkedIn, Metro Manila location) are also dual-track. Google rewards pages that match the searcher's intent; when two intents live on one page, H1 clarity matters more than keyword density. The dual-path pattern is battle-tested: Stripe, Linear, Vercel, and Gumroad all use variants of it on their homepage today.

Why this direction

  • Dual-audience reality — Your real ICP is both. Don't artificially narrow the funnel on the highest-traffic page.
  • SEO coverage — Captures both "build me an AI product" and "integrate AI into my company" keyword clusters without splitting into two sites.
  • Conversion hygiene — Each visitor lands in the track that matches their language. Bounce rate drops, time-on-page climbs, both boost SEO.
  • Future-proofing — As the agency scales, each track can deepen into its own sub-page (/products, /ai-integration) without a rebrand.

The trade-off: Option C sacrifices a small amount of hero-copy punch compared to A or B. If your pipeline is heavily skewed (80%+) toward one audience in the next 3–6 months, pick that lane instead — specificity converts better than breadth.

Open question for you: Of your last 10 inbound conversations, how many were founders vs. business owners? That ratio decides whether we go Dual Path (C) or commit to one lane (A or B).
